Use case: 3D reconstruction reference sheet. The two views get fed to a multi-image
reconstruction service, so they must be the SAME OBJECT seen from two angles, drawn in one image.

Asset type: an object turnaround sheet — two views of one timber-framed wall panel, in a single row.

Style rules: match the attached reference exactly in material language and atmosphere. This is the
MUSE-UM pavilion idiom: hand-drawn European graphic-novel rendering, medieval epic and ancient sacred
architecture, ethereal, a forgotten civilization remembering itself beneath a dying sun. Warm aged
oak with dark grain and tool marks, pale bone-coloured plaster, muted blue-grey shingles, thin
engraved linework. Matte hand-finished surfaces, no plastic sheen, no chrome, no neon, no gloss.

The object: one rectangular wall panel of the French Tudor kind, standing upright like a section of
wall cut out and set on the ground. Heavy hand-cut dark oak posts and rails form the outer rectangle.
Inside it, a single vertical central stud runs the full height, and two thick diagonal braces run
from the corners up to that stud, forming a shallow V. The spaces between the timbers are filled with
flat pale plaster, slightly uneven and hand-troweled. On top of the frame sits a narrow sloping head
of small overlapping blue-grey shingles, like the bottom course of a roof, with a thin oak capping rail
beneath it. The timbers are thick and clearly structural — much wider than they are deep in appearance
— with visible grain, adze marks, and darkening in the joints. Rectangular overall, taller than it is
wide, roughly two and a half times as tall as it is wide.

The plaster infill must read as OPAQUE SOLID SURFACE with its own matte texture. There must be no
transparency, no glazing, and nothing to see through anywhere in the object.

Keep the ornament BOLD AND SIMPLE — broad readable shapes and strong timbers, not fine filigree. Fine
detail smears when the image is reconstructed into geometry.

Views, left to right, all the same object at the same scale and the same height in frame:
  1. front elevation, looking straight at the face of the panel
  2. three-quarter view, turned roughly forty degrees so the thickness of the timbers and the depth
     of the frame are clearly visible

Composition: the two views evenly spaced across one horizontal sheet, separated by a clear empty gap
so they can be cut apart. Identical scale and identical lighting in both views. Each view upright,
fully inside the frame with generous empty margin on all sides. No overlap, no touching edges.

Use the LARGEST canvas you can produce, and make the panel as large within the frame as the margins
allow — every extra pixel of the object survives into the reconstruction.

Background: perfectly uniform pure magenta #FF00FF at every pixel outside the object, with no shadow,
no floor, no gradient, no vignette. The object must be cleanly separable from the background.

Constraints: one object only. Absolutely no text, lettering, numerals, labels, arrows, measurement
marks, captions, borders, or watermarks anywhere in the image. No second object, no scenery, no
figures, no ground, no wall behind it.
