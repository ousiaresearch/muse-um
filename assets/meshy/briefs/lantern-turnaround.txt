Use case: 3D reconstruction reference sheet. The two views get fed to a multi-image
reconstruction service, so they must be the SAME OBJECT seen from two angles, drawn in one image.

Asset type: an object turnaround sheet — two views of one hanging lantern, in a single row.

Style rules: match the attached reference exactly in material language and atmosphere. This is
the MUSE-UM pavilion idiom: hand-drawn European graphic-novel rendering, medieval epic and ancient
sacred architecture, ethereal, a forgotten civilization remembering itself beneath a dying sun.
Warm aged brass with dark patina in the recesses, muted bone and parchment, thin engraved linework.
Matte hand-finished surfaces, no plastic sheen, no chrome, no neon, no glossy highlights.

The object: a single hanging lantern of the kind carried through this pavilion. A hexagonal cage of
slim warm-brass uprights with a large circular hanging ring at the top, a narrow tapered hexagonal
roof, a short brass finial at the base, and a narrow band of fine engraved hatch marks around the
widest part of the frame. Aged and hand-made, not machine-perfect.

CRITICAL — the glazing: the six body panels must be OPAQUE FROSTED GLASS, milky and cloudy, like
hammered or aged glass that you cannot see through. Draw them as SOLID VISIBLE SURFACES with their
own soft matte sheen and slightly irregular hand-made edges, clearly a different material from the
brass. Do NOT draw them as open frames, bare openings, or clear transparent panes: a still image
cannot show transparency, so transparent or empty glazing is lost entirely when the image is
reconstructed into 3D geometry.

Keep the ornament BOLD AND SIMPLE — broad readable shapes, not fine filigree. Fine detail smears
when the image is reconstructed into geometry.

Views, left to right, all the same object at the same scale and the same height in frame:
  1. front elevation, showing one full frosted panel centred
  2. three-quarter view, turned roughly forty-five degrees, showing a corner post near the centre
     and two frosted panels receding

Composition: the two views evenly spaced across one horizontal sheet, separated by a clear empty
gap so they can be cut apart. Identical scale and identical lighting in both views. Each view
upright and fully inside the frame with room to spare, leaving generous empty margin on all sides.
No overlap, no touching edges.

Use the LARGEST canvas you can produce, and make the lantern as large within the frame as the
margins allow — every extra pixel of the object survives into the reconstruction.

Background: perfectly uniform pure magenta #FF00FF at every pixel outside the lantern, with no
shadow, no floor, no gradient, and no vignette. The lantern must be cleanly separable from the
background.

Constraints: one object only. Absolutely no text, lettering, numerals, labels, arrows, measurement
marks, captions, borders, or watermarks anywhere in the image. No second object, no scenery, no
figures, no floor, no wall, no candle flame.
